(10.0.0.1) Piso WiFi Pause Time: How to Pause, Check & Resume Your Session

If you run or use a coin-operated Piso WiFi vendo machine, Piso WiFi Pause Time is the feature that lets you freeze your remaining internet minutes instead of losing them when you step away. This guide walks through what pause time is, how to open the pause time panel at 10.0.0.1, and how to fix the most common problems people run into.

What is Piso WiFi pause time?

Piso WiFi vendo machines are coin- or app-based internet vending units common in sari-sari stores, apartments, and small businesses across the Philippines. A user inserts coins (or pays through a linked e-wallet) and receives a block of internet minutes. Piso WiFi pause time is a built-in feature of the vendo software that lets a connected user temporarily suspend the countdown on their purchased time, so stepping away from the WiFi signal — going home for the night, leaving a store, or simply not using the internet for a while — doesn't burn through paid minutes.

Instead of the timer running continuously from the moment of payment, pause time creates a "banked" balance. The remaining minutes sit untouched until the same device reconnects and the user actively resumes the session. This is one of the most requested features among vendo machine owners because it directly affects customer satisfaction: nobody likes paying for wifi minutes they never actually used.

Why 10.0.0.1? Most Piso WiFi vendo boxes run a small local web server on the private address 10.0.0.1, which is only reachable while your phone or laptop is connected to that specific machine's WiFi network. It is not a public website — you cannot open it from mobile data or from a different WiFi network.

How to access 10.0.0.1/pause

Before anything else, your device needs to actually be joined to the vendo machine's WiFi network. Piso WiFi networks are usually named something like "PISOWIFI", "PisoNet", or a custom name chosen by the machine owner, and typically don't require a WiFi password to join — payment happens after connecting, inside the browser portal.

  1. Connect to the vendo WiFiOpen your device's WiFi settings and join the vendo machine's network. You do not need internet access yet to do this.
  2. Open a browserLaunch Chrome, Safari, or any browser and type 10.0.0.1 directly into the address bar — not a search engine box.
  3. Wait for the portal to loadThe vendo machine's captive portal should load automatically. If it doesn't, try adding http://10.0.0.1/ with the http:// prefix explicitly.
  4. Locate the pause optionOnce you're logged into an active session, look for a "Pause" or "Pause Time" button on the portal's main screen, usually next to the countdown timer.

Pausing your session step by step

Once you can see the vendo portal with your active countdown timer, pausing is usually a single tap, but the exact placement of the button varies slightly by the vendo software version installed on the machine (common builds include LPB, Pisowifi Pause Time Ultimate, and various customized forks).

  1. Open the portal at 10.0.0.1Confirm you can see your remaining minutes on screen.
  2. Tap "Pause"This is typically a orange or red button beside the timer display, sometimes represented by a pause icon (⏸).
  3. Confirm the promptSome builds show a confirmation dialog asking "Pause your session now?" — tap yes or confirm.
  4. Note your remaining balanceThe portal usually displays your frozen remaining time (e.g., "58 minutes left") so you can track it later.
  5. Disconnect if neededYou can now safely leave WiFi range, turn off WiFi, or move to another network. Your paused balance stays reserved for your device.

Important: Pause time is almost always tied to your device's MAC address. If you reconnect using a different phone, or your device's MAC address changes (common on some Android phones with "private WiFi address" turned on), the machine may not recognize you as the same user, and your paused balance may not carry over.

Resuming a paused session

To pick up where you left off, reconnect to the exact same vendo WiFi network using the same device you paused from.

  1. Reconnect to the networkJoin the same Piso WiFi network as before.
  2. Revisit 10.0.0.1Open your browser and go back to the portal address.
  3. Tap "Resume" or "Unpause"Your frozen countdown should reappear with the same remaining minutes you had when you paused.
  4. Verify the timer is runningWatch the countdown for a few seconds to confirm it has resumed ticking down normally.

If the resume button is missing or your balance shows zero, see the troubleshooting section below — this is almost always caused by a device or network change rather than the vendo machine actually deleting your minutes.

Checking remaining time and data

Most Piso WiFi portals display two numbers while you're connected: remaining minutes (or hours) and, on some machines, a remaining data cap in MB or GB. To check without pausing, simply open 10.0.0.1 in a browser while connected — the balance is shown on the landing screen without needing to log in again, as long as your session is still active and your device hasn't been idle long enough to trigger an automatic timeout.

Fixing common pause time problems

Pause button does nothing

Refresh the page at 10.0.0.1, confirm you're still connected to the vendo WiFi (not a saved but out-of-range network), and try again. A weak signal can cause the pause request to silently fail.

Balance reset to zero after pausing

This usually means the machine's software doesn't retain pause state across a full device reboot, or your device connected with a rotated MAC address. Try disabling "private/random WiFi address" for this network in your phone's settings.

Portal won't load at all

Forget the network and rejoin it, restart WiFi on your device, and make sure you're typing the address into the URL bar and not a search box. Some browsers auto-redirect http to https and break the captive portal — try a different browser if this happens.

Resume button missing

Some vendo builds hide the resume option after a set number of hours to prevent abuse. If a long time has passed since you paused, contact the machine owner — many can manually restore a balance from the admin panel.

Frequently asked questions

Does pause time work if I switch phones?

Usually not. Pause time is linked to the MAC address of the device that paused the session, so switching devices typically starts a fresh, unpaused session instead of restoring the old balance.

How long can I leave a session paused?

This depends entirely on how the specific vendo machine is configured by its owner. Some allow indefinite pausing, others automatically clear paused balances after 24–72 hours of inactivity.

Will pausing protect my data allowance too, not just time?

On machines that enforce a data cap alongside a time limit, pausing generally freezes both, but this varies by the software build installed — some older versions only pause the timer.

Can the machine owner see that I paused?

Yes. Owners can typically see all active and paused sessions from the admin panel, including device identifiers and remaining balances.

What is the LPB vendo system?

LPB refers to a particular Piso WiFi vendo firmware/software combination that many small operators install on a Raspberry Pi or mini PC connected to a router and a coin acceptor or bill validator. It provides a customer-facing captive portal (where users pay and manage sessions) and a separate password-protected admin dashboard (where the machine owner manages pricing, network settings, and reports). The customer-facing side is reached at 10.0.0.1 once connected to the machine's WiFi.

Compared to some older vendo scripts, LPB is popular because its pause time button is prominent on the main screen rather than buried in a menu, and because it tends to log session history in a way that's easy for owners to review at the end of the day.

Logging into the LPB user portal

  1. Join the vendo WiFi networkLook for the network name posted on or near the coin machine.
  2. Open 10.0.0.1 in your browserType it directly into the address bar; the LPB portal should appear automatically as a captive portal.
  3. Insert coins or select a rateFollow the on-screen prompts — LPB typically shows a rate table (e.g., ₱5 for 30 minutes, ₱10 for 1 hour) before you insert payment.
  4. Wait for the timer to startOnce payment registers, your countdown timer appears at the top of the portal along with the pause button.

Tip: LPB portals usually remember your device for a short window even after you close the browser tab. If you reopen 10.0.0.1 while still in range and your session hasn't expired, you'll be dropped straight back into your active timer without needing to pay again.

Using pause time on LPB

On the LPB interface, the pause control sits directly beneath the countdown clock as a labeled button. Tapping it locks in your remaining balance and shows a "Paused" badge. To resume, reopen the same portal from the same device and tap the button again, which now reads "Resume."

Accessing the LPB admin dashboard

The admin dashboard is a separate, password-protected area meant only for the machine owner or operator — not for regular WiFi customers. It is normally reached through a distinct path or port from the same base address, such as an /admin route, and is protected by a username and password set during installation.

  1. Connect to the same local networkAdmin access generally still requires being on the vendo's own network or a direct connection to its management interface.
  2. Navigate to the admin pathThis is usually appended after the base address, for example 10.0.0.1/admin. The exact path can differ by installation.
  3. Enter your admin credentialsUse the username and password configured when the machine was set up. If you never changed them, check the installer's documentation or the reseller who configured your unit — default credentials are never something a public guide should publish, since publishing them would only help someone break into a machine that isn't theirs.
  4. Reset forgotten credentials safelyIf you're the legitimate owner and have lost your password, most LPB installations support a credential reset via direct access to the underlying device (Raspberry Pi/mini PC) rather than through the web portal, for security reasons.

Key admin settings explained

Rate table

Set how many minutes or hours each coin denomination buys. Most owners tier this so bulk payments (₱20, ₱50) are proportionally better value than single coins.

Pause window

Controls how long a paused session is preserved before it's automatically cleared, often configurable from a few hours up to several days.

Bandwidth limits

Caps the speed or data volume per connected device so one user can't monopolize the shared connection.

Session logs

A running record of connections, payments, and pause events, useful for reconciling coin totals against reported usage.

Troubleshooting LPB-specific issues

Coins register but the timer never starts

This is typically a coin-acceptor pulse miscount rather than a software fault — check the physical coin mechanism's wiring and pulse setting in the admin panel matches the coin denomination in use.

Portal loads but shows a blank white screen

Often caused by a browser caching an old version of the portal page. Clear the browser cache for that site or try a private/incognito window.

Admin dashboard won't accept the correct password

Confirm caps lock is off and that you're on the exact admin path used during setup — some installs are case-sensitive on both username and route.

Multiple devices show as connected but only one paid

Check whether "free trial minutes" or a guest-access setting is enabled in the admin panel; some LPB configurations grant a small free window before payment is required.

Frequently asked questions

Is LPB the same as every Piso WiFi machine?

No — LPB is one specific vendo software package among several in circulation. The general pause-time concept is shared across most of them, but menu layout, admin paths, and default settings differ by build.

Can I install LPB myself?

Installation is typically handled by resellers or hobbyists who set up the Raspberry Pi/mini PC hardware and coin mechanism together; it's a hardware-plus-software project rather than an app you install on an existing router.

Why 192.168.1.254 and not 192.168.1.1?

Both addresses are common defaults, but different manufacturers pick different conventions. Some brands place the router/gateway at the "low" end of the subnet (192.168.1.1), while others — including many ISP-supplied modems and several Piso WiFi vendo configurations — place it near the "high" end at 192.168.1.254, reserving that address as the last usable host on a standard /24 subnet. Neither is more "correct"; it simply depends on how the device was configured out of the box.

The "192.168.l.254" typo, explained

A huge number of people searching for this address accidentally type a lowercase letter L in place of the number 1, because on many fonts and keyboards they look almost identical — "192.168.l.254" instead of "192.168.1.254". Browsers treat these as completely different, invalid addresses, and the page will fail to load with a "can't reach this page" or "server not found" error. If your login attempt fails instantly with no login screen at all, this typo is the single most common cause — double-check that every character is the number one, not a letter.

Quick check: Numeral 1 usually has a small flat serif or hook at the top and a flat base in most browser address-bar fonts; the letter L is a plain vertical stroke. When in doubt, delete the address and retype it manually rather than trusting autocomplete or a copy-pasted link from an untrusted source.

Step-by-step login

  1. Connect to the right networkJoin the WiFi network (or plug in via Ethernet) for the specific router or vendo box you're trying to configure.
  2. Open a browser and type the address exactlyEnter 192.168.1.254 into the address bar, not a search engine.
  3. Wait for the login promptYou should see a username/password prompt, either a browser popup or a styled login page depending on the device.
  4. Enter your credentialsUse the credentials specific to your device — often printed on a sticker on the router itself, or provided by whoever installed the vendo machine.
  5. Land on the dashboardA successful login brings you to a settings dashboard where network, pricing, or WiFi options are configured depending on the device type.

Finding your actual gateway address

If you're not certain your device even uses 192.168.1.254, you can confirm the real gateway address directly from your computer or phone rather than guessing.

What to do if the page won't load

Wrong network

Confirm you're connected to the specific router or vendo network in question, not a different WiFi network or mobile data.

HTTPS redirect issues

Some browsers force https:// automatically. Try explicitly typing http://192.168.1.254 with the http prefix.

Address is actually different

Use the gateway-check steps above; some devices ship on 192.168.0.1, 192.168.1.1, or 10.0.0.1 instead.

Browser cache conflicts

Clear cached data for that address or try a private/incognito window, especially if you've logged into a different router at the same address before.

Basic security once you're in

Once logged into any admin dashboard at a private address like this one, treat it the same as any other sensitive account. Change any default username and password to something unique, avoid reusing a password from another account, and log out of the dashboard when finished rather than leaving the session open on a shared device.

Frequently asked questions

Is 192.168.1.254 a public website?

No. It's a private, local network address that only resolves to something when you're connected to the specific router or vendo device using it — it means something different on every network.

Why does my browser say "not secure"?

Local admin panels usually run over plain HTTP rather than HTTPS since they're only reachable on a private local network, so browsers flag them as not secure. This is expected behavior for this kind of local device login, not a sign of a public data breach.

I typed it correctly but still see an error page

Try restarting the router or vendo device, waiting a minute for it to fully boot, then reconnecting your device to its network before trying the address again.

Getting the address format right

A standard IPv4 address always has exactly four numbers separated by three dots, each between 0 and 255 — for example 10.1.1.1. A search like "10.1.1.1.0/admin" adds an extra segment that makes it technically invalid as a plain address; what's usually meant is the base address 10.1.1.1 followed by a path, such as 10.1.1.1/admin (no extra zero, one slash before the word "admin"). Typing the invalid five-segment version into a browser will simply fail to resolve.

Correct format: http://10.1.1.1/admin — four numbers, three dots, then a single forward slash before any page path.

Logging in step by step

  1. Connect to the correct networkJoin the WiFi network or plug in via Ethernet to the specific device using this address.
  2. Type the base address firstEnter 10.1.1.1 alone into your browser to confirm the device responds at all.
  3. Proceed to the admin path if promptedIf the base address shows a general status page rather than a login form, try appending /admin to reach the configuration login.
  4. Enter your credentialsUse the username and password specific to your device or vendo installation.
  5. Confirm dashboard accessA successful login should present network, pricing, or device settings depending on what kind of hardware you're logging into.

Reaching the admin panel

On devices that use this address, the admin panel is generally kept separate from any customer-facing pause-time or payment portal, precisely so that regular WiFi users browsing the network can't stumble into configuration settings by accident. If you're a regular customer just trying to use paid WiFi rather than configure the device, you likely don't need the admin path at all — look instead for whatever landing page appeared automatically when you first connected.

Admin login vs. customer portal

The same base address, 10.0.0.1, often serves two different experiences depending on the path you visit and whether you're already authenticated as the owner. Regular customers land on the pause-time and payment portal automatically. Owners and administrators instead need a distinct login path — commonly something like 10.0.0.1/admin — protected by credentials that are separate from anything a paying customer ever sees.

Not the same thing: If you're only trying to pause or check your paid WiFi time as a customer, you want the main portal, not the admin login. See the pause time guide instead.

About the "10.0.0.0.1" typo

"10.0.0.0.1" has five number segments, which isn't a valid IPv4 address — real IPv4 addresses always have exactly four segments separated by three dots. This typo most often comes from someone accidentally typing an extra "0." while trying to write 10.0.0.1 quickly, or from a search engine autosuggestion getting garbled. If a page you're on shows this exact string, correct it to the four-segment version before trying again.

  2. Navigate to the admin pathEnter the specific admin URL set up during installation, such as 10.0.0.1/admin.

  4. Review the dashboardOnce logged in you should see session history, rate settings, and network configuration options.
  5. Log out when finishedEspecially important if the management device is shared or located somewhere customers can physically access it.

If you're locked out

Losing admin access to a machine you legitimately own is frustrating but recoverable in most cases. Because publishing generic default usernames and passwords publicly would primarily help someone access a machine that isn't theirs, this guide intentionally doesn't list them. Instead, legitimate owners locked out of their own hardware typically have these options:

  • Check any setup documentation, sticker, or install notes left by whoever configured the machine originally.
  • Contact the reseller, installer, or vendo software provider directly — most keep records tied to the specific unit's serial number.
  • If you have physical and direct access to the underlying hardware (Raspberry Pi or mini PC), a local factory-reset procedure is usually available through the device itself rather than the web interface.

Keeping the admin panel secure

Since anyone briefly connected to the customer WiFi network is technically on the same local network as the admin panel, it's worth treating admin credentials with real care rather than leaving factory defaults in place. Change the default password immediately after setup, use a password not reused anywhere else, and if your vendo software supports it, restrict the admin path to a separate management network or VLAN rather than the open customer WiFi.

Only if they know or guess the specific admin path and have valid credentials — simply connecting to the WiFi and visiting the base address routes regular users to the customer portal, not the admin login.

Is 10.0.0.1 the same on every vendo machine?

The address itself is common, but the software running behind it, the admin path, and the exact features available differ by vendo build — always refer to your specific installation's documentation where possible.

Can I access the admin panel remotely from outside the WiFi range?

Not by default, since 10.0.0.1 is a private address only meaningful on that machine's own local network — remote access would require additional configuration such as a VPN back into that network, set up deliberately by the owner.

Why this site exists

Searching for a bare IP address like 10.0.0.1 or 192.168.1.254 usually returns a mess of outdated forum posts, conflicting instructions, and pages that don't distinguish between logging in as a paying customer versus logging in as the machine's owner. This site tries to separate those two audiences clearly, keep instructions general enough to apply across different vendo builds, and flag the common typos (like a stray letter L instead of the number 1) that quietly break people's login attempts.

What we cover

  • Pausing, checking, and resuming a paid Piso WiFi session.
  • Logging into common admin/gateway addresses used by vendo machines and small routers.
  • Distinguishing customer-facing portals from owner-only admin dashboards.
  • Practical troubleshooting for the login and connectivity issues people report most.

What this site is not

This is not an official resource from any router manufacturer or Piso WiFi vendo software developer, and it does not publish default admin credentials for any device. Exact menus, button placement, and default settings vary by manufacturer and by the specific vendo build installed on a given machine — always confirm details against your own device where it matters.

Our approach to accuracy

Private network addresses and vendo software both change over time, and no single guide can perfectly describe every build in circulation. Where behavior varies by device or software version, we say so explicitly rather than presenting one version as universal. If you notice a page that no longer matches what you see on your own machine, the contact page is the place to flag it.
